# Common Ground — field atlas art direction

Original assets for the November 2026 website redesign. Runtime assets live in this directory; nothing references a developer's machine.

- `common-ground-atlas.webp`: custom generated editorial illustration, 1536 × 1024, ~480 KB. Generated with the built-in image generation tool, then encoded to WebP. A visual metaphor, not a photograph of an actual installation.
- `mark.svg`: original vector mark. Two circles share a vermilion intersection above a ground line.
- `connection.svg`: original vector colophon. Two engraved contour forms connected by one red line.

Design system: warm paper `#f4f1e9`, charcoal `#272923`, vermilion `#ac3928`; Instrument Serif for display titles, Geist for controls and readable prose, restrained monospace for figure labels. Real HTML remains selectable and accessible. Essential forms and dashboard data are never painted into images. The dashboard stays operational and quiet. Existing photo assets retain their source credits.

## Exact generation prompt

Use case: stylized-concept. Asset type: original editorial hero illustration for Common Ground, a software factory that shares useful engineering tools between ocean and solar companies. Create a refined horizontal 3:2 fine-art photogravure collage on warm uncoated ivory paper. A carefully composed aerial coastline: dark detailed ocean waves on the left, a narrow estuary across the middle, geometric rows of solar panels and agricultural fields on the right. One very fine vermilion survey line connects a tiny ocean research vessel to a tiny industrial building inland, suggesting knowledge traveling between industries. Topographic contour marks, subtle halftone ink, delicate engraved detail, restrained antique scientific atlas aesthetic with contemporary art direction. Mostly monochrome charcoal and warm cream with only a single restrained red line. Strong graphic composition, beautifully tangible printing texture, generous unmarked paper margins, slightly irregular cut edges of the geographical illustration. No words, no letters, no typography, no UI, no logos, no cards, no glowing nodes, no gradients, no sci-fi holograms, no 3D isometric blocks. A bespoke museum-quality editorial illustration, not a generic stock technology background.
